Public budgets and contracts are, in principle, open. In practice they are inaccessible: hundreds of pages of fund-accounting tables, inconsistent fiscal years, nominal-vs-real confusion, and contract dumps with no narrative. The people most affected — residents, local journalists with no data desk, students, and newly-elected officials with no staff — cannot read them. The gap is not transparency; the data is published. The gap is **comprehension**.
